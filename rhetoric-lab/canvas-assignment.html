11/19 — The Rhetoric Lab: How King's Words Work

Today you will work on your own in The Rhetoric Lab, a website that teaches how King's language works and then asks you to explain it in your own words, using chunks 2 and 3 of "I Have a Dream."

Open these two tabs

  • The Rhetoric Lab: Open The Rhetoric Lab
  • Your text: your I Have a Dream interactive notetaker (the copy you annotated Wednesday) [TEACHER: LINK THE NOTETAKER HERE]. Chunk 2 is paragraphs 7–13. Chunk 3 is paragraphs 14–23.

What to do

  1. Check in on the lab's first page.
  2. Clear the five gates in order: 1 The Toolbox · 2 The Image Lab · 3 The Sound of Persuasion · 4 The Evidence Test · 5 Your Analysis. Stuck? Show a hint and the worked explanations are allowed. Press Listen to hear any section read aloud.
  3. Gate 5 is your own writing: responses A, B and C, with evidence from both chunks. Save about 12 minutes for it.
  4. Go to Your Report. Type your name and class period, select Generate my report, then Copy Report.
  5. Come back to this assignment, paste the report into the Text Entry box (Ctrl+V), and select Submit Assignment.

Your report is your graded submission. It shows your practice and your analysis, and Mr. Moody grades your explanations in A, B and C. A complete report has all five gates cleared. If time runs out, submit the report you have: it shows how far you got. Finishing the website does not submit anything; only submitting here does.

If something goes wrong

  • Copy Report doesn't work: use Download .txt or Print / Save as PDF in the lab, then upload the file here.
  • Switching Chromebooks: on the old one, select Save my work to a file. On the new one, select Load my work from a file.
  • Can't open your notetaker: the lab's Class copy button links the Yale transcript and shows where chunks 2 and 3 start and end.
  • The website won't open: use the Printable Fallback attached to this assignment. Complete A, B and C in your spiral under today's title and upload clear photos. If you can't upload, keep the dated page ready for Mr. Moody.

Work independently. You may use your notetaker, your annotations and the lab's Reference. Copying another student's analysis is not allowed.
