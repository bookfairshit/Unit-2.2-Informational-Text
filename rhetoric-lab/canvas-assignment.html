11/19 — The Rhetoric Lab: How King's Words Work

Today you will work independently through The Rhetoric Lab. You will practice explaining figurative language, rhetorical devices, appeals, and support, then analyze King's speech in your own words.

The lab has five gates. Each gate opens the next. You can build your report at any time, and it shows which gates you have cleared. Clear all five for a complete report, and that report is your graded submission for this assignment.

Rhetoric Lab website: [TEACHER: PASTE THE RHETORIC LAB LINK HERE]

Your text, the I Have a Dream notetaker: [TEACHER: LINK THE NOTETAKER HERE] · Chunk 2 is paragraphs 7–13; Chunk 3 is paragraphs 14–23.

  1. Open the linked Rhetoric Lab website.
  2. Keep your notetaker open to chunks 2 and 3 in another tab. You may use your annotations and the site's reference tools.
  3. Check in, then clear Gates 1–4: short instruction and practice. Read the feedback and revise when needed. Hints and worked explanations are allowed and recorded.
  4. Clear Gate 5: final responses A, B, and C in your own words, with evidence from both chunks. If you cannot open your notetaker, use the Yale transcript linked in the site's Class copy panel and check Transcript used.
  5. On the Report page, select Generate my full report, then Copy Report. Paste the report into this Canvas assignment's Text Entry box and submit. If copying fails, download the text file or use Print → Save as PDF and submit through the enabled file-upload option.

Your final work should explain how the words work, not just name a device or appeal. Completing the website does not automatically submit anything to Canvas.

If the website will not open, use the posted student fallback and your notetaker. Complete A, B, and C in your spiral under the same lesson title, then submit legible photos if possible. If you cannot upload, keep the dated work ready for Mr. Moody to collect.
