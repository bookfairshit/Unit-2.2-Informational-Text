The Rhetoric Lab: How King’s Words Work

Students work independently on a website linked in Canvas, reading chunks 2 and 3 of their I Have a Dream notetaker (a Google Doc). The lab has five gates; each opens the next. Students can build their report (their graded submission) at any time, and it shows how many gates they cleared, so a late student can still build and print one. You do not need to teach rhetoric: the website teaches. Please help with access and navigation only.

Opening script (read aloud)

“Open today’s Rhetoric Lab assignment in Canvas and follow the website directions. Work independently. Keep your speech copy open: your I Have a Dream notetaker, chunks 2 and 3. There are five gates. Clear as many as you can; your report shows how far you got. You will submit that report to Canvas before the end of class. The website does not submit it for you.”

While students work

  • Check that each student is responding on their own device or dated paper.
  • Students may use the glossary (the Reference button) and their own annotations. They should write their own explanations.
  • No group spokesperson today.
  • Help with navigation and access; the website provides the instruction.

Timing

Gate 5 reminderAbout 26 minutes into a 40-minute class, 33 minutes into a 47-minute class, or 36 minutes into a 50-minute class. Say: “If you are not in Gate 5 yet, use Show a hint and the worked explanations to clear your gates faster. Save about 12 minutes for Gate 5.”
Last two minutesSay: “Build your full report and submit it in Canvas. A completion screen on the website is not a Canvas submission. Not finished? Build and submit the report you have; it shows how far you got. Your work also stays saved on this device.”
Fast finishersRevise one weak explanation using evidence → meaning → effect → purpose, then try the optional extension at the end of Your Analysis. No unrelated activity before they submit.

If something goes wrong

Website will not openRefresh once, then hand out the printed student fallback. Students complete A, B, and C in their spiral under the lesson title and upload photos if they can.
Can’t open the notetakerThe site’s Class copy button links the Yale transcript and shows where chunks 2 and 3 start and end; students check Transcript used. With no internet at all, hand out the printed chunks 2–3 and the printed fallback if they were left. Do not supply quotations from memory.
Copy Report does nothingClick in the report box, press Ctrl+A, then Ctrl+C. Or use Download .txt or Print / Save as PDF and upload the file.
“Not saving” messageKeep the tab open and copy or download the report before closing it. Work is saved only in the browser where it was typed.
Changed devicesOld device: Report page → Save my work to a file. New device: Load my work from a file.
Reading supportThe Listen buttons read instructions and questions aloud; headphones help.
“My gate won’t open”The locked screen lists exactly what is left. Every task can be finished: after two tries, the worked explanation and Continue with support count.
Shared deviceAfter submitting, the student can use Erase my work on this device on the Submit page.
A student is upsetLet them work quietly and leave a note below.

What students submit: one report pasted into the Canvas assignment’s Text Entry box (or an uploaded .txt/PDF, or photos of spiral work). Preserve whatever work students complete.
