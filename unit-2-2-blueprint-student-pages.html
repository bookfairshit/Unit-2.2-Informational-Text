The Text Blueprint: how a text gets built

An author’s purpose, appeals and language aren’t separate. They work together to build the text.

The buildingEffect

What does the audience think, feel, and do?

Mood is the feeling the words create. The effect should lead back to the purpose.

MaterialsLanguage

Which words do the work?

Metaphor, simile, repetition, imagery, allusion, word choice.

FrameAppeals

How will the author reach this audience?

Ethos: credibility and character (why trust?). Logos: logic and evidence (does it make sense?). Pathos: emotion (how does it feel?). Strong texts combine them.

FoundationPurpose

Why does this text exist?

P persuade · I inform · E entertain or engage. The primary purpose is the main destination; texts often inform or entertain in order to persuade.

Blueprint sentence___’s primary purpose is to ___. To reach ___, the author combines ___ and ___, using ___, so that the audience ___.

Read the blueprint from the bottom up: purpose is the foundation everything else stands on.

Station A · Foundation: Purpose

Find King’s purposes and show how they work together.

Discover

  1. Find one place King INFORMS (facts or history).
  2. Find one place he PERSUADES (calls for belief or action).
  3. Find one place that ENGAGES (a vivid picture or story that pulls listeners in).

Explain · in your spiral

  1. “King informs about ___ in order to ___.”
  2. “King’s primary purpose is to persuade ___ to ___.”
  3. Stretch: “The support that best proves his point is ___ because ___.”

Done when you have

  • Three lines found (inform, persuade, engage), with opening words
  • Your own primary-purpose sentence
  • The Checker initialed your passport
Use your transcript and write the opening words of each line you use. Before you move, the Checker reads every member’s work and initials the passport. A group answer is not your answer.

Station B · Frame: Appeals

Show how King combines ethos, logos and pathos to reach his audience.

Discover

  1. Find one line for each appeal: ethos, logos, pathos. Write its opening words.
  2. Which appeal carries the promise section? Which carries the dream section?

Explain · in your spiral

  1. For each appeal: “King uses ___ in ‘___’ to make his audience ___, which helps him ___.”
  2. “He shifts from ___ to ___ because ___.”

Done when you have

  • One line for each appeal, with opening words
  • An explain sentence for each appeal
  • Your shift sentence
  • The Checker initialed your passport
Use your transcript and write the opening words of each line you use. Before you move, the Checker reads every member’s work and initials the passport. A group answer is not your answer.

Station C · Materials + Effect: Language and mood

Connect King’s word choices to the appeal they power and the mood they create.

Discover

  1. Hunt for 3 devices: metaphor, simile, repeated phrase, light vs. dark image, real place name, historical or biblical allusion, call to action.
  2. For each, name the mood it creates (hopeful, urgent, determined, defiant, solemn, joyful…).

Explain · in your spiral

  1. For each device: “The ___ in ‘___’ powers ___ and creates a ___ mood, so the audience ___.”

Done when you have

  • Three devices, each tied to an appeal AND a mood
  • Three explain sentences
  • The Checker initialed your passport
Mood words: hopeful · urgent · determined · defiant · solemn · indignant · joyful · triumphant · peaceful
Use your transcript and write the opening words of each line you use. Before you move, the Checker reads every member’s work and initials the passport. A group answer is not your answer.

In your spiral, draw both boxes. Use one excerpt from each section of the speech.

Excerpt A — Promise

  • Words the exact words
  • Language the device or word choice
  • Appeal name it and say what it means
  • Purpose persuade whom, to do what
  • Effect think, feel, do + the mood

Excerpt B — Dream

  • Words the exact words
  • Language the device or word choice
  • Appeal name it and say what it means
  • Purpose persuade whom, to do what
  • Effect think, feel, do + the mood
Then finish with your blueprint sentence for the whole speechKing’s primary purpose is to ___. To reach ___, he combines ___ and ___, using ___, so that his audience ___.

Strong checkpoints connect the layers. “Pathos” alone is a label; say which feeling, in whom, and how it serves King’s purpose.
